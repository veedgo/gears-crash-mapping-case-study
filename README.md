# GEARS Crash Mapping & Validation

**Georgia Electronic Accident Reporting System (GEARS)**  
**Georgia Department of Transportation**  
**Appriss → LexisNexis Risk Solutions / Coplogic Solutions**  
**Remote contract work | 2016–2017**

## Summary

This case study documents remote crash-data mapping and validation work performed for GEARS, a statewide crash-data repository supporting the Georgia Department of Transportation.

Across **45 documented invoices**, the work totaled **20,865 record actions**:

- **12,677** latitude/longitude assignments
- **6,416** private-property classifications
- **1,641** manual validations
- **131** unable-to-locate classifications

The work combined high-volume structured data processing with geospatial reasoning, evidence reconciliation, anomaly detection, and judgment under incomplete or conflicting source information.

## Production and Quality

The highest documented production week contained **851 completed record actions**. That same week received a **100% accuracy audit score**.

Project correspondence also documents:

- an **85% weekly accuracy requirement** for invoice approval
- repeated **100% audit results**
- other scores above the required threshold
- continued assignment of increasingly difficult records
- explicit trust in independent location judgment

## Nature of the Work

Typical responsibilities included:

- assigning crash locations by latitude and longitude
- manually validating crash records
- determining whether crashes occurred on private property
- identifying cases where evidence could not support a defensible location
- interpreting roadway names, intersections, ramps, access roads, directions, distances, and landmarks
- comparing police-report evidence with maps and satellite imagery
- reconciling incomplete or conflicting location information
- investigating roadway, map-layer, and address discrepancies
- escalating systematic or unresolved mapping issues

## Selected Problem-Solving Examples

### Recurring road-name discrepancy
Multiple reports referred to **Matt Keene Circle**, a roadway not visible in the available mapping data. The recurring pattern was identified and escalated with multiple examples; the technical team later supplied roadway access-point information.

### Map-layer discrepancy
Records involving **Sewell Mill Road and Pine Road** did not align cleanly with Google Maps. The issue was escalated, satellite imagery was reviewed, and GDOT provided placement guidance.

### Historical roadway geometry
An older crash near **Pleasant Hill Road and I-85** could not be reconciled with the present roadway configuration. The likely explanation was later roadway reconstruction, and specific placement guidance followed.

### Changing business addresses
A crash report contained a business address that differed from the address returned through Google, raising the possibility that historical relocations were contributing to mapping confusion across multiple records.

## Increasing Responsibility

Later in the project, GDOT requested concentrated work on unresolved Cobb County records involving **fatality or injury crashes**, including records in statuses such as Pending Manual Mapping, Failed Validation, and Skipped.

At that stage, the project manager explicitly communicated trust in independent judgment regarding where those crashes had taken place and asked for the best defensible placement unless the evidence genuinely remained insufficient.

## Corporate Transition

The engagement began under **Appriss**. During 2016, LexisNexis Risk Solutions acquired the Appriss Crash and Project business. Correspondence and invoice formatting later reflected **LexisNexis / Coplogic Solutions**, while the GEARS work continued.
