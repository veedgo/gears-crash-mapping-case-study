## Featured / Project Post

One of the older projects I recently reconstructed from surviving records turned out to be substantially more interesting than the one-line description I had remembered.

From 2016–2017, I worked remotely on the **Georgia Electronic Accident Reporting System (GEARS)** through Appriss and later LexisNexis Risk Solutions, supporting crash-data mapping and validation for the Georgia Department of Transportation.

The surviving records document:

- **20,865 record actions**
- **12,677 latitude/longitude assignments**
- **1,641 manual validations**
- **851 actions in the highest-volume week**
- **100% audit accuracy during that same peak week**

The work often went beyond routine mapping. Crash reports could contain incomplete, conflicting, outdated, or ambiguous location information, requiring reconciliation across roadway descriptions, maps, satellite imagery, distances, landmarks, and historical context.

Later in the project, I was assigned difficult fatality/injury records that had failed or resisted previous validation, with explicit trust from the project manager to make the best defensible location judgment possible.

I’ve now reconstructed the project as a portfolio case study, with the public version deliberately excluding crash-report details and other sensitive source material.
